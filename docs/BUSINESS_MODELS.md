# Business Ideas & Models

The goal is not to find an idea that sounds futuristic. The goal is to find a narrow problem where an agent can create measurable economic value.

## Opportunity score

Score each idea 1–5 on:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Pain | Does the problem cost meaningful money, time, risk, or lost revenue? |
| Frequency | Does it happen weekly or more often? |
| Budget | Is there an identifiable buyer with budget? |
| Access | Can you reach 100 prospects cheaply? |
| Data | Are inputs available digitally and legally? |
| Agent edge | Can an agent materially outperform a simple rules engine or manual workflow? |
| ROI proof | Can value be measured in 30 days? |
| Retention | Will the workflow repeat and become sticky? |

**40 points max.** Prefer ideas scoring 30+ before investing heavily.

## 12 promising agent-business patterns

### 1. Revenue follow-up agent
**Customer:** small B2B teams with inbound leads.
**Pain:** leads go cold because reps do not follow up consistently.
**Offer:** qualify, draft personalized follow-ups, schedule next steps, and flag hot leads.
**Pricing:** $500–$2,500/month or per qualified meeting.
**Why it works:** clear revenue linkage and repeated workflow.

### 2. Proposal / RFP copilot
**Customer:** agencies, contractors, consultancies, enterprise sales teams.
**Pain:** proposal creation consumes expert hours and delays responses.
**Offer:** ingest prior work, requirements, pricing rules, and produce compliant drafts with review checkpoints.
**Pricing:** subscription + onboarding or per proposal.

### 3. Accounts receivable agent
**Customer:** service businesses with overdue invoices.
**Pain:** cash is trapped in receivables.
**Offer:** monitor invoices, prioritize collection risk, send compliant reminders, prepare escalation actions.
**Pricing:** monthly fee + optional percentage of recovered cash.

### 4. Local-business front desk agent
**Customer:** dentists, med spas, repair shops, salons, home services.
**Pain:** missed calls and slow replies lose bookings.
**Offer:** answer FAQs, capture intent, qualify, book, and follow up.
**Pricing:** setup fee + monthly subscription + usage.

### 5. Compliance evidence agent
**Customer:** software companies pursuing security/compliance programs.
**Pain:** recurring evidence collection is manual and annoying.
**Offer:** collect, map, summarize, and flag missing evidence while keeping humans in approval loops.
**Pricing:** annual or monthly SaaS.

### 6. Customer support resolution agent
**Customer:** software/ecommerce companies.
**Pain:** support teams spend time on repetitive, low-complexity tickets.
**Offer:** resolve bounded issues, draft replies, update records, and escalate uncertainty.
**Pricing:** per resolved ticket or platform subscription.

### 7. Recruiting sourcing agent
**Customer:** staffing firms and internal recruiting teams.
**Pain:** finding and researching candidates is labor intensive.
**Offer:** identify candidates, enrich context, score fit, and draft outreach for human approval.
**Pricing:** seat, role, or successful-intro fee.

### 8. Competitive intelligence monitor
**Customer:** product, sales, strategy, and investor teams.
**Pain:** competitors, pricing, launches, hiring, and messaging change constantly.
**Offer:** monitor defined sources and deliver decision-ready changes, not link dumps.
**Pricing:** $200–$5,000+/month depending on scope.

### 9. Sales-call QA agent
**Customer:** high-volume sales teams.
**Pain:** managers cannot review enough calls.
**Offer:** score calls against a rubric, identify coaching moments, and track trends.
**Pricing:** per seat, hour, or call.

### 10. Vertical document workflow agent
**Customer:** insurance, logistics, real estate, legal ops, construction, finance ops.
**Pain:** documents move slowly between inboxes, PDFs, portals, and systems.
**Offer:** extract, compare, validate, route, and draft follow-ups with human exceptions.
**Pricing:** per document/workflow + platform fee.

### 11. Research-as-a-service agent
**Customer:** investors, agencies, executives, niche operators.
**Pain:** high-value research consumes analyst time.
**Offer:** recurring market maps, account research, due diligence support, or lead intelligence with sources.
**Pricing:** retainer or subscription.

### 12. Agent infrastructure
**Customer:** teams building production agents.
**Pain:** reliability, evals, permissions, cost control, observability, memory, and deployment are difficult.
**Offer:** solve one painful infrastructure layer exceptionally well.
**Pricing:** usage + platform fee.

## Best first model: productized service

If an agent founder has zero customers, a productized service is often the best wedge because it produces revenue and proprietary workflow knowledge before software is finished.

Example:

> “We recover stale B2B leads for agencies. We connect to your CRM, identify neglected opportunities, prepare personalized follow-up sequences, and deliver qualified re-engagement opportunities. $1,500/month.”

Behind the scenes, humans and agents can do whatever is necessary to deliver the result. Once the same steps repeat across customers, automate them.

## Next model: AI-native managed outcomes

Once a productized service has a measurable completion condition and repeatable workflow, consider turning it into an **AI-native managed service / micro-BPO**: sell the completed business job, not access to an agent.

The dedicated playbook covers process qualification, outcome contracts, pricing, human + agent staffing, gross-margin math, 30-day paid pilots, and concrete AR, RFP, and support-operations blueprints:

- [`AI_NATIVE_MICRO_BPO.md`](AI_NATIVE_MICRO_BPO.md)
- [`../templates/OUTCOME_MANAGED_SERVICE_OFFER.md`](../templates/OUTCOME_MANAGED_SERVICE_OFFER.md)

A useful progression is:

**founder-operated service → agent-assisted productized service → managed outcome service → configurable platform → API/agent capability**.

Do not productize faster than the evidence supports.

## Alternative path: acquire existing demand

A founder does not have to start with zero customers. Another model is to **buy, partner with, or become the operating layer behind an existing service business**, then use agents to improve the workflows serving an already-established customer base.

Use the dedicated acquire-and-automate playbook to screen targets, separate existing business value from unproven automation upside, preserve tacit operating knowledge, run pre-commitment workflow proofs, plan the first 100 days, and decide whether building, partnering, or acquiring is actually justified:

- [`ACQUIRE_AUTOMATE_SMB.md`](ACQUIRE_AUTOMATE_SMB.md)

The key rule is simple: **do not pay today for automation savings that only exist in a future-state spreadsheet.**

## Avoid these traps

- “AI for everyone” with no narrow buyer.
- A feature that is useful but not budget-worthy.
- Consumer products that require huge distribution before revenue.
- Businesses dependent on scraping or data access you cannot legally or reliably sustain.
- Fully autonomous high-stakes decisions without appropriate human review.
- A marketplace with neither side already available.
- Building an agent platform before you know which workflow customers value.
- Acquiring a weak business because an AI story makes the spreadsheet look better.

## Pick your wedge

Write one sentence:

> We help **[specific customer]** achieve **[measurable outcome]** by handling **[painful recurring workflow]**, and we charge **[pricing model]**.

If the sentence is vague, the business is still vague.
