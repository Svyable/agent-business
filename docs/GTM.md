# Go-To-Market Playbook

Early distribution should answer one question: **can you repeatedly reach people with this problem and move them toward a paid outcome?**

## Start with one segment and one channel

Do not begin with “content + SEO + ads + partnerships + outbound.” Pick one.

Good early channels for agent businesses:

- founder-led outbound to a narrow prospect list,
- niche communities and associations,
- integration/technology partners,
- service partners already trusted by the buyer,
- founder-led content aimed at a specific painful workflow,
- marketplaces where the buyer already searches for help,
- warm introductions from adjacent vendors or customers.

## Build a 100-account list

A good list is narrow enough that the same pain hypothesis applies to most accounts.

Capture:

- account,
- buyer role,
- visible pain signal,
- current tool/workaround,
- trigger event,
- contact path,
- status,
- next action.

Examples of trigger events:

- hiring for a repetitive workflow,
- fast team growth,
- new compliance requirement,
- backlog complaints,
- expansion into a new market,
- public pricing/product change,
- increased inbound volume.

## Outreach formula

Keep first contact short:

1. specific observation,
2. likely business problem,
3. credible outcome,
4. low-friction next step.

Example:

> Saw that your team is hiring two coordinators for intake. We’re testing an agent-assisted intake workflow that handles the repetitive qualification and routing work while keeping edge cases with humans. For teams with similar volume, the target is to cut response time and coordinator load. Worth comparing notes for 15 minutes?

Avoid fake familiarity, invented claims, automated impersonation, and deceptive urgency.

## Sell the pilot

A strong pilot has:

- one workflow,
- one owner,
- one success metric,
- a fixed start/end date,
- a paid price,
- required customer inputs,
- explicit conversion terms if successful.

Example:

> 30-day pilot for one support queue. Success = at least 30% of eligible tickets resolved without human drafting while maintaining agreed QA thresholds. $2,000 pilot, then $1,500/month + usage if successful.

## Founder-led sales process

1. **Discovery** — verify pain, urgency, current workflow, budget owner.
2. **Diagnosis** — quantify cost and identify constraints.
3. **Demo** — show their workflow, not generic AI capability.
4. **Pilot proposal** — narrow scope and measurable outcome.
5. **Close** — confirm commercial and implementation details.
6. **Launch** — get the customer to first value quickly.
7. **Review** — show outcome data and decide expansion/conversion.

## Landing page structure

1. Headline: measurable outcome for a specific customer.
2. Problem: show you understand the current painful workflow.
3. Solution: explain what changes, in plain language.
4. Proof: data, pilot result, customer quote, or transparent demo.
5. How it works: 3 simple steps.
6. Pricing or pricing logic: reduce uncertainty.
7. Trust: data handling, human review, limitations, security where relevant.
8. CTA: one clear next step.

## Distribution loops worth building later

After early sales work:

- customer referrals,
- reusable integration ecosystems,
- templates users share,
- benchmark reports that attract the target market,
- proprietary data network effects,
- partner channels,
- community or marketplace liquidity,
- product-led invites where collaboration is natural.

## Weekly GTM scorecard

Track:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Qualified accounts added | pipeline quality |
| Personalized touches | activity |
| Positive replies | message/segment resonance |
| Discovery calls | actual demand conversations |
| Proposals/pilots offered | commercial movement |
| New paid customers | revenue proof |
| Time to first value | activation |
| Expansion/referrals | value strength |

If activity is high but conversations are low, fix targeting/message. If conversations are high but sales are low, fix pain severity, offer, proof, or price.
