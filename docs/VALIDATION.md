# Validation Playbook

Validation means collecting evidence that a specific customer has a painful problem and will spend money to solve it.

## 1. Define a testable customer/problem pair

Bad: “small businesses need AI.”

Better: “5–30 person HVAC companies lose booked jobs because after-hours calls are not answered quickly.”

Write:

- Customer:
- Painful event:
- Current workaround:
- Cost of the problem:
- Buyer:
- Trigger that makes them seek a solution:

## 2. Find 20 prospects

Build a small list manually. Useful sources include public company directories, professional communities, niche forums, LinkedIn, local directories, associations, and people already in your network.

For each prospect, capture only what is needed to contact and learn:

- company/person,
- why they fit,
- likely pain evidence,
- contact path,
- status,
- notes.

Do not automate bulk spam. Personalize outreach and respect applicable platform rules and laws.

## 3. Run problem interviews

The goal is not to pitch. Ask about real past behavior.

Questions:

1. Walk me through the last time this problem happened.
2. What did you do to fix it?
3. How often does it happen?
4. What happens if it is not solved?
5. Roughly what does that cost in time, money, risk, or lost revenue?
6. Who owns the problem?
7. What tools or services have you already tried?
8. What did you dislike about them?
9. Is solving this a priority this quarter? Why or why not?
10. If a solution reliably produced **[outcome]**, how would you evaluate whether it was worth paying for?

Record exact phrases. Those phrases become landing-page and sales copy later.

## 4. Ask for a commercial commitment

Strong validation signals, from strongest to weaker:

1. paid pilot or deposit,
2. signed contract,
3. letter of intent with price and timeline,
4. scheduled procurement/security step,
5. introduction to the actual budget owner,
6. explicit willingness to pay a stated price,
7. repeated usage of a manual prototype,
8. “this is cool.”

The last one is not validation.

## 5. Sell a concierge version first

Deliver the outcome manually with agent assistance before building a full product.

Example:

- customer forwards a batch of documents,
- you process them using agents + human review,
- deliver structured output in their existing workflow,
- track time, errors, edge cases, and value,
- then automate the repeated steps.

This reveals where the real product boundary belongs.

## 6. Decide after 10 conversations

Continue if you see several of these:

- the same pain appears repeatedly,
- buyers quantify meaningful cost,
- customers already spend money/time on workarounds,
- at least one buyer accepts a paid test,
- you can reach more prospects through the same channel,
- delivery can reach healthy gross margin after learning.

Change direction if:

- everyone likes it but nobody prioritizes it,
- every customer needs a completely different solution,
- the buyer has no budget authority,
- required data cannot be accessed reliably,
- value cannot be measured,
- acquisition cost will likely exceed expected lifetime gross profit.

## 7. Minimum validation dashboard

Track weekly:

| Metric | Target for early validation |
|---|---:|
| Prospects contacted | 20–50 |
| Replies | 5+ |
| Interviews | 5+ |
| Strong pain confirmations | 3+ |
| Offers made | 2+ |
| Paid pilots | 1+ |

Targets are directional, not universal. The important signal is whether real buyers keep moving closer to a purchase.

## 48-hour challenge

Within the next 48 hours:

1. pick one customer/problem pair,
2. identify 20 prospects,
3. send 10 personalized messages,
4. conduct at least 2 conversations,
5. make one paid pilot offer.

Do not add product features during this challenge.
